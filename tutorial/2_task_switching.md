# Step 2. Task switching

## The analysis

Two questions now:

- Is a response slower right after a switch of task than after a repeat? That
  difference is the **switch cost**.
- Is the switch cost different for congruent and incongruent trials?

## The experiment

The same words in the same ink colours. A cue says what to do on each trial:
**name the colour** or **read the word**.

## Ask your assistant

> Now there are two tasks on the same stimuli: name the ink colour, or read the
> word. I will compare switch trials with repeat trials, and I want to know
> whether the switch cost differs between congruent and incongruent trials.
> Extend the design and show me the counts.

## What to check

- **Is the switch a factor?** Whether the task switches depends on the trial
  before. Count switch and repeat trials for each task and each congruency.
- **The first trial.** It has no trial before. What did the design do with it?
- **What else repeats?** When the task repeats, does the response repeat more
  or less often than when it switches? Count it. Did the assistant raise it?
- **Would shuffling have done it?** Ask the assistant what a shuffled list of
  the same trials gives for switch and repeat.

## The idea

What happened on the trial before can be a factor of the design like any
other: a transition factor. Then it can be crossed with the rest. Whatever is
not in the crossing is left to chance, and it is worth counting.
