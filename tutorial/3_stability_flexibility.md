# Step 3. Stability and flexibility

## The analysis

Staying on a task against distraction (stability) and moving to another task
(flexibility) pull against each other. You want to see that trade-off by
changing the context in **mini-blocks** and measuring both effects in each:

- the **congruency effect**: how much distraction gets through;
- the **switch cost**: how hard it is to change task.

There are two ways to change the context. Pick one, or do both.

| Way | The mini-blocks differ in | The question |
|---|---|---|
| A | how many trials are congruent: mostly congruent or mostly incongruent | When conflict is frequent, is the switch cost larger? |
| B | how often the task switches: rarely or frequently | When switches are frequent, is the congruency effect larger? |

## Ask your assistant

For way A:

> I want mini-blocks of about 16 trials. In some, three quarters of the trials
> are congruent; in others, one quarter. In every mini-block I will measure the
> switch cost and the congruency effect. Design the two kinds of mini-block and
> a session of four of them, and show me the counts.

For way B, replace the second sentence:

> In some, the task switches on three quarters of the trials; in others, on one
> quarter.

## What to check

- **The proportions.** Are they what you asked for, in every mini-block?
- **The cells.** In a mostly congruent block, how many incongruent switch
  trials are there? Is that enough to measure a switch cost for them?
- **What gave way?** A short block cannot balance everything. What did the
  assistant leave out of the crossing, and did it tell you? Count the ink
  colour.
- **Short blocks and the trial before.** A mini-block is a short stretch of
  trials that is balanced inside. What does that do to what follows what?
  The second example on the website is about exactly this.

## The idea

An imbalance can be the manipulation: here it is a weight on a level. And
there is often no design without a cost, only a choice between costs. Which
cost is acceptable depends on what you compare, so the analysis comes first.
