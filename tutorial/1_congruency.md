# Step 1. Congruency

## The analysis

Is naming the ink colour of a word slower when the word names another colour?
You compare **congruent** trials (the word RED in red) with **incongruent**
trials (the word RED in green).

## The experiment

A Stroop task with three colours: red, green and blue. On each trial one of
the three words is shown in one of the three ink colours. Participants name
the ink colour.

## Ask your assistant

> I want to run a Stroop task with three colours. I will compare response times
> on congruent and incongruent trials. Design a block of 24 trials for me and
> show me the counts.

## What to check

- **How many trials are congruent?** With three colours, showing every word in
  every ink colour equally often makes one third of the trials congruent. Did
  the assistant ask which you want, or did it choose?
- **What is balanced?** Count ink colour by congruency. Then count the word:
  it follows from the other two. Is it even?
- **Why 24?** Which block lengths keep the design balanced?

## The idea

Congruency is not something you set. It follows from the ink colour and the
word: a derived factor. Only what is crossed is balanced, so if you compare
congruent with incongruent trials, congruency has to be in the crossing.
