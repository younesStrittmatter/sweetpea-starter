    A distractor that is usually in one place

    Source: Gong & Theeuwes (2021), Attention, Perception, & Psychophysics, Experiment 3. doi:10.3758/s13414-020-02142-8

    Takeaway. Often there is no design without a cost, only a choice between costs, and which cost is acceptable depends on the analysis. Ask which comparisons are planned before proposing a design. When two things cannot share a level (a place, an item, a key), the level that one of them occupies most cannot also be an ordinary level for the other. Lay out the options with what each keeps and gives up, say which fits the planned comparisons and why, and write the chosen design down so that design and analysis agree.

    Research question

    What, and why. Attentional control: people learn where a salient distractor tends to appear and suppress that location. Is the suppression of a location fixed, or does it adjust to how salient the distractor on the current trial is?

    How. Visual search for a diamond among eight shapes. On most trials another shape stands out too (the distractor), weakly or strongly, most often at one location. Suppression is read from how much a distractor slows the search at its frequent location, compared with other locations and with no distractor.

    Part used. A distractor at the frequent location against no distractor.

    Analysis

    Response time with the distractor at its usual place against response time without a distractor, within participants.

    Design as published

    Decision Chosen Reason given What follows
    Distractor Absent on 1 trial in 6; otherwise at one place on 65% of the trials, at each of the 7 others on 5% The manipulation: people are to learn the place Unequal on purpose
    Target without a distractor Equally often at each of the 8 places Stated At the distractor's usual place on 1 trial in 8
    Target with a distractor At random among the 7 places the distractor does not occupy Stated With the distractor at its usual place, never at that place

    What to balance

    Assumption. Trials with the distractor at its usual place and trials without a distractor differ only in the distractor.

    Where it fails. They differ in where the target can be. The target is at the distractor's usual place on 1 trial in 8 without a distractor, on 1 in 7 with the distractor elsewhere, and never with the distractor at that place.

    Those trials are in the design on purpose: the paper compares targets at that place with targets elsewhere, to show that the place is suppressed, and finds them slower. The problem is not that they exist. It is that the comparison with "no distractor" counts them as ordinary baseline trials. This is a mismatch between design and analysis, not an accident of the design.

    This is a regular factor (the place of the target), not a derived one. It is the simplest case of a combination that cannot exist.

    Options

    Three wishes cannot all hold: the distractor is usually at one place (the manipulation); target and distractor are never at the same place; and where the target is tells nothing and is told by nothing. The options differ in how often the target is at the distractor's usual place (design(choice) in design.py). Each keeps one wish and gives up two.

    Choice Target at the usual place Keeps Gives up
    "as often" as at any other free place (the published design) 1 trial in 8 without a distractor, 1 in 7 with the distractor elsewhere; 9% of all trials A distractor elsewhere says nothing about the target The usual place holds the target less often than the other places; 1 baseline trial in 8 has its target where the compared condition never has it
    "never" never Every trial has the target elsewhere, so the baseline can use all its trials A place that never holds the target can be learned as such; no trial is left to compare targets there with targets elsewhere
    "more often", only on trials with the distractor at another place 1 in 8 without a distractor, as in the original; more with the distractor elsewhere (twice as often in the code; 10 to 7 gives exactly 1 in 8 over all trials) Every place holds the target equally often; the baseline is as in the original With a distractor elsewhere, the target is likelier at the usual place than anywhere else

    The third option leaves the trials without a distractor alone, because they are the baseline of the main comparison. In SweetPea it is a second copy of some trials: a factor copy in the crossing, and an Exclude of every second copy except those with the target at the usual place and the distractor elsewhere.

    Which design for which analysis

    No option is best in general. Which fits depends on the comparisons that are planned (best_for(comparisons) in design.py). This is reasoning from the counts, not a result of the paper.

    Planned comparison What it needs Fits Why
    Distractor at its usual place against no distractor The same target places in both "never", or any option with the baseline formed from the trials with the target elsewhere With the distractor at its usual place the target is always elsewhere
    Distractor at its usual place against distractor at another place The same "never", or the others restricted to the target elsewhere; "more often" leaves fewest usable trials Trials with the distractor elsewhere can have the target at the usual place
    Target at the usual place against target elsewhere, without a distractor Trials with the target at the usual place "as often"; "more often" if targets being rare at that place is itself a worry "never" has no such trials

    So: if only the first two are planned, "never" uses every trial, at the price of a place that never holds the target. If the third is planned, as in the paper, "never" is out, and the baseline of the other comparisons has to leave out the trials with the target at the usual place.

    In every option the trials with the distractor at its usual place have the target at the seven other places. A baseline that is fair to them is made of trials that have the target at those seven places: target_place == "elsewhere".

    What to present

    Ask first which comparisons are planned. Then show all three options with the tables above, say which fits those comparisons and why, and name what it gives up. Do not present one as correct in general. Write the chosen design down with target_place as a factor, so that the choice is explicit and the analysis can follow it.
