    Simon task: the trial before

    Source: Dunaway & Weissman (2026), Psychological Research, Experiment 4. doi:10.1007/s00426-025-02216-y

    Takeaway. Making things even within a short stretch of trials (sets, mini-blocks) changes what follows what: a trial then follows its own type, its side and its response less often than half of the time, and this does not even out over a block. When a user asks for such sets, say so, and offer to make what repeats from the trial before part of the design. The sets can stay.

    Research question

    What, and why. Cognitive control: when two irrelevant signals are present at once, do their effects on a response add up or interact? The answer speaks to whether one control process handles all distraction or several handle different kinds.

    How. A prime word (Red, Green or Cross) is followed by a red or green square on the left or right. Participants press the key of the colour. The word can match the colour or not; the side can match the side of the key or not (the Simon effect). The two effects are compared across their combinations.

    Part used. The Simon effect and its interaction with prime compatibility.

    Analysis

    Response time and errors by prime compatibility (compatible, neutral, incompatible) and Simon congruency (congruent, incongruent), within participants.

    Design as published

    Decision Chosen Reason given What follows
    Combinations 3 words x 2 sides x 2 colours = 12 Fully crossed design Each of the 6 conditions twice per set
    Sets A block is 9 sets of the 12 combinations, each set in its own random order "By presenting each of the 12 possible combinations equally often, we avoided contingency learning confounds." Inside a set a trial follows its own type less often than another type
    Trial before Not controlled None A congruent trial follows a congruent one in 46% of cases (11/24), not 50%

    The design is counterbalanced for the combinations, by choice and for a stated reason. The trial before is a side effect of that choice.

    What to balance

    Assumption. The compared conditions have the same kinds of trial before them. Congruency effects depend on the trial before (the congruency sequence effect), and so do repetitions of the side and of the response.

    Where it fails. Inside a set, whatever was just shown is scarcer among the trials that are left. Between two sets nothing is biased. The borders are fair, and fair cannot make up for the bias inside the sets, so it does not even out over a block. It concerns every property that is even within a set, derived or not.

    What repeats from one trial to the next, over many blocks of shuffled sets:

    Options

    In the design (functions in design.py):

    Option Sets kept What repeats Cost
    proposed: sets of 12, plus "the side repeats" crossed with "the colour repeats" yes trial type 50%, side 50%, response 50%, same square 25% One extra first trial per block (109). Also even within every four trials, which is stricter than needed. Seconds to generate.
    proposed_block_level: the same, even over the block only yes the same About five minutes to generate.
    conditions_crossed: each of the 6 conditions follows each 3 times yes trial type 50% About two minutes; side and response are not addressed.
    trial_type_only: Simon congruency crossed with that of the trial before yes trial type 50%, side and response further from 50% than before Evens out one property by adding trials on which side and colour both change. Do not propose.
    No sets: cross the square with the square before no all even Gives up the sets and what they were for.

    In the analysis, with the published design:

    Option Cost
    Add the trial before as a factor of the analysis More cells with fewer trials each; the first trial of a block drops out.
    Reweight the conditions to the same share of each kind of trial before Changes what the condition mean estimates; needs a choice of weights.

    What smaller sets cost. Sets and an even trial before are two demands on the same order. With sets of 12 no further restriction was found. With sets of 4 (each square once) every set has to start with the square that ended the last one, because a square can repeat only across a border. With sets of 2 the order of trial types is fixed (c i i c c i i c). Say this when a user wants small sets.

    None of the options is the correct one; which to take depends on the claim.

    What to tell the user

    When someone asks for sets, mini-blocks or "each condition once every n trials", say these four things before writing the design:

    1. Sets make every combination equally frequent and evenly spread. They also make a trial follow its own type less often than half of the time, and that does not even out over a block. Give the number for their set size.
    2. It concerns everything that is even within a set: the trial type, the side, the response, the stimulus itself.
    3. It matters when the measure depends on the trial before, which is usual in conflict, switching and priming tasks. Ask whether it does.
    4. It can be made part of the design with the sets kept. Evening out one property alone makes the others worse, so balance all of them, and say what small sets cost.

    Design to propose

    proposed(). Raise what repeats from the trial before with the user, show this design, and name the alternatives with their costs.

    It uses two crossings in one MultiCrossBlock: the three stimulus factors, repeated set by set (mode=RepeatMode.REPEAT), and two Transition factors, "the side repeats" and "the colour repeats", crossed with each other. The repeat mode applies to every crossing of the block. For a count over the whole block only, use Repeat with ExactlyK on one transition factor, as in proposed_block_level().
